Unitree Q3 2026 Earnings Preview: Five Things to Watch in Its First Report Since the IPO

On October 29, Unitree Robotics (688836.SH) publishes its third-quarter results — the first earnings report of its life as a public company. The headline numbers still look strong, but the details on margins, humanoid mix and cash deployment will set the tone for the humanoid-stock sector into 2027. Here is the complete preview, built only on verifiable public data.

By China Robotics Insider · Oct 22, 2026 · Last updated: Oct 22, 2026 · 10 min read
Share on X LinkedIn
The preview · TL;DR
Unitree's Q3 report lands October 29. H1 2026 showed revenue of ¥1.152B (+48.5%) and net profit of ¥274M, but net profit excluding non-recurring items fell 19.3% and gross margin slipped to 56.0% from 60.4% in FY2025. The Q3 2025 comparison base is ¥391M; broker forecasts imply Q3 2026 revenue of roughly ¥660–740M (+70–90%). Five things to watch: (1) humanoid mix after the G1+ upgrade, (2) whether margins stabilize, (3) the R&D ramp into the AI "brain," (4) use of the ~¥6.1B IPO cash, and (5) the valuation fight — JPMorgan Underweight (TP ¥300) versus domestic Buy ratings. At ¥495.92, the stock trades at ~7.3x 2026 estimated sales. Editorial research, not investment advice.

Unitree enters its first earnings season as a public company in an unusual position: operationally dominant, financially profitable, and yet surrounded by a market debate that has already cut its share price by roughly half from the opening print. The October 29 report will not resolve the argument about whether humanoid robots are a generational market or a priced-to-perfection theme, but it will give the first independently audited quarterly data points of the post-IPO era. This is what to look for.

Why the October 29 report matters

Unitree listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange's STAR Market on August 19, 2026 (688836.SH), the first Chinese humanoid-robot maker to list on a mainland exchange. Its IPO price was 150.80 yuan; the stock opened at 1,100 yuan, up 629%, and then spent the next two months re-rating downward. The Q3 report covers July–September 2026 — a quarter that straddles the IPO — and is the first set of results judged by public-company standards of disclosure, including quarterly cash flow, segment detail and, typically, management commentary on the second half.

The two numbers that deserve attention are the 19.3% decline in recurring (扣非) net profit despite 48.5% revenue growth, and the gross-margin slide. Both trace back to the same forces: product price cuts, a faster ramp in spending, and a large base of non-recurring gains in the prior-year period. The Q3 report will show whether those are one-off listing-year effects or a trend.

Watch 1: Humanoid mix after the G1+ refresh

Humanoids became Unitree's largest line in 2025, contributing 51.78% of revenue, with 5,511 units shipped (global No.1, ~32% world share) at a 63.2% segment gross margin. The Q3 catalyst came on September 14, when the G1 was upgraded to the G1+: six upgrades across motion, perception and interaction, priced at 95,000 yuan incl. tax — below the original G1's 99,000 yuan launch price. Cumulative humanoid production had reached roughly 18,000 units by July, per the company's sales materials.

What to look for: the share of revenue from humanoids versus quadrupeds, the split between consumer/research sales and emerging industrial pilots (H1/H2 units are being tested on production lines and in logistics), and any order-book or delivery commentary around the G1+. The unit-volume trajectory matters more than ever because the market is effectively valuing Unitree on 2027–2028 scale.

Watch 2: Margins — the 60% question

Unitree's margin story is the most distinctive in the industry, and the most misunderstood. Blended gross margin rose from 44.2% (2023) to 56.7% (2024) and 60.1% (2025), even as it cut prices — the result of full-stack in-house actuator production and scale. But humanoid-specific margins have been moving the other way: 87.7% (2023) → 68.4% (2024) → 62.9% (Jan–Sep 2025), as the lower-cost G1 ramped and prices fell. H1 2026 blended margin was 56.0%.

What to look for: whether Q3 gross margin stabilizes in the mid-50s or continues to drift. A stable-to-up quarter (helped by the Q3 2025 base and rising H2 volume) would support the bull case that scale offsets price cuts; a print below 55% would sharpen the bear case that humanoid margins converge toward ordinary hardware levels as competition intensifies.

Watch 3: The R&D ramp into the "brain"

Unitree's early R&D concentrated on the body and the "cerebellum" (motion control). R&D spending was ¥49.95M (2023), ¥70.02M (2024) and ¥144.97M (2025) — 31.39%, 17.83% and 8.53% of revenue respectively. Since 2024 the company has been deliberately strengthening the "brain" side, including AI foundation-model work. Speaking at the 2026 World Physical AI Conference on October 22, founder Wang Xingxing said Unitree is pre-researching a physical-AI robot self-evolution V1.0 model, with significant technical breakthroughs targeted for 2027–2028.

What to look for: the absolute R&D run-rate (it should be rising materially from the 2025 quarterly average), capitalization policy, and any disclosed headcount or model milestones. Rising R&D is strategically necessary but will weigh on operating margin in the near term — expect it to be a key line of questioning.

Watch 4: Cash, IPO proceeds and cash flow

The STAR Market IPO raised about ¥6.1B (against an initially planned ¥4.2B), leaving Unitree with a balance sheet unlike any of its private peers. The prospectus earmarked proceeds for production capacity, next-generation R&D and working capital. The first post-IPO report will show how much cash remains, capital expenditure into the Hangzhou capacity build, and the operating-cash-flow conversion behind the headline profit — a useful check on the quality of earnings given the 扣非 divergence.

Catalysts around the print

  • H2 Plus with NVIDIA — the June 2026 reference design pairs the H2 humanoid with Jetson Thor and the Isaac GR00T platform; launch is expected in the second half of 2026.
  • H2-X "Kung Fu Legend" — announced October 21, with up to ~8 hours of battery life, extending the full-size line.
  • Physical-AI model V1.0 — pre-research disclosed October 22; the "brain" narrative for 2027–2028.
  • Policy backdrop — China's first six national humanoid-robot standards were released in October (effective April 2027), shaping the commercialization framework.

Frequently asked questions

When does Unitree report Q3 2026 earnings?

Unitree Robotics (688836.SH) is scheduled to release its third-quarter 2026 results on October 29, 2026 — the first earnings report since listing on the STAR Market on August 19.

What were Unitree's H1 2026 results?

Revenue of 1.152 billion yuan (+48.54%), net profit attributable to shareholders of 274 million yuan (versus a 32 million yuan loss a year earlier), but net profit excluding non-recurring items fell 19.34% to 244 million yuan and gross margin eased to 56.01%.

What is Unitree's stock price and market cap?

Unitree closed at 495.92 yuan on October 19, 2026 (+16.15%), with a market cap of about 200.6 billion yuan. It had touched a post-listing low of 404.03 yuan on October 15, versus an opening price of 1,100 yuan on debut and an IPO price of 150.80 yuan.

What are analyst targets for Unitree?

JPMorgan: Underweight, target 300 yuan (December 2027). Nomura: target 370 yuan. Huaxin Securities: Buy, with 2026–2028 revenue forecasts of 2.76B, 4.54B and 6.87B yuan. CCB International: 2026 target market cap of 109 billion yuan.

Is Unitree a good investment?

Unitree is the world's largest humanoid shipper with unusually high hardware margins, but growth is decelerating, humanoid margins are falling, and the stock trades at roughly 7x 2026 estimated sales. Analyst views are sharply split. Size any position for high volatility and rely on official filings — this article is editorial research, not investment advice.
