Hefei, China's Inland Robot Factory: Who Moved Production There, and What Is Real

Geek+, YouiBot, LimX Dynamics, X Square and KUKA China have opened bases in an inland city better known as the bet behind NIO, BOE and ChangXin Memory. Nearly 200 robot and embodied-AI companies have clustered there, but the list mixes finished-product plants, module factories, integration centers and R&D offices. This piece separates what the supply chain and policy actually provide from what remains a pilot.

By China Robotics Insider · Nov 25, 2026 · 11 min read
Share on X LinkedIn
The short version
Hefei, the capital of Anhui province in eastern China, says it has gathered nearly 200 robot and embodied-AI companies covering the full chain from model brain, core parts and body to scenario deployment. City figures put 2025 humanoid output above 400 units and industrial robots at 22,000 sets, up 1.1 times year on year; local media roundups put 2024 smart-robot output above RMB 50 billion. The anchor arrivals are Geek+ (2590.HK), which built its Lujiang manufacturing headquarters and sole global production base for heavy and high-speed picking robots, and YouiBot, which moved its headquarters and smart-manufacturing base to Feidong. A wider list spans LimX Dynamics, EngineAI, Leju, X Square, Yiyou Zhikong, Weiyi, KUKA China and a humanoid JV called Dingli Kesin. The honest caveat: most companies are still fundraising, sampling or in small batches; several projects make joint modules or do integration and assembly, not whole robots; and humanoid mass production has not been cracked anywhere in China yet.

Hefei is not the first city overseas observers associate with Chinese robotics. Shenzhen owns the hardware supply chain and a stack of startup headquarters; Hangzhou hosts Unitree; Beijing concentrates model labs and institutes; Shanghai draws embodied-AI research centers. Hefei's pitch is different. It is an inland manufacturing city that grew up around cars, home appliances, displays and semiconductors, and it is trying to turn those factories' parts, engineers and open scenarios into a robot cluster. The playbook is the one that backed NIO, BOE and ChangXin Memory earlier: municipal state capital enters early, supply-chain partners follow, and the payoff is judged over a decade rather than a quarter.

The numbers behind the current push come from the city government's October 2026 manufacturing roundup: nearly 200 robot and embodied-intelligence companies across "brain, cerebellum, core components, body and scenario application"; over 400 humanoid units produced in 2025; 22,000 sets of industrial robots, up 1.1 times. At the September 2026 World Manufacturing Convention in Hefei, more than 20 local robot and embodied-AI companies exhibited 13 humanoid robots on stage.

The two anchor arrivals: Geek+ and YouiBot

The best-documented outside commitments are two mobile-robot companies that moved not just an office but a manufacturing base into the city.

Geek+ (2590.HK) signed its Lujiang project in the He-Lu industrial new city in November 2022 with a reported total investment of RMB 6.1 billion, and the first phase went into production in November 2024. The site covers 90 mu (about 72,000 m²) and is described by the company and local government as the only global production base for its heavy-payload and high-speed picking robots, with annual capacity of 20,000 units/sets; later design capacity after full build-out is quoted at 200,000 units. Local government reports put 2025 output at RMB 1.02 billion. Geek+ listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in July 2025 and is described in company and media materials as the global AMR share leader for seven consecutive years; that ranking is a company/media claim rather than an audited third-party figure.

YouiBot, the Xi'an Jiaotong University-spinoff in wafer-handling, mobile manipulation and inspection robots, opened its headquarters and smart-manufacturing center in the Feidong Economic Development Zone in November 2024. The base has a clean production workshop of more than 7,000 m², with roughly 400 employees and more than a third in R&D; new-product development and volume production are anchored in Hefei. The company reports 800-plus scenario deployments and 400-plus global customers, and cites itself as the global number-one mobile-manipulation robot vendor by 2024 revenue — again a company-attributed ranking. Chinese media report YouiBot is pursuing an IPO, with a 18C-style Hong Kong filing mentioned in some reports and a securities-registry record noted in others; the exchange and board are not settled, so this piece treats it as an IPO in progress rather than a listed name.

The wider list: joint modules, humanoid teams and an integration center

A second wave of arrivals spreads across Hefei's districts. The spatial layout the city uses is a "4+3" pattern: Yaohai, Baohe, Shushan and the High-tech Zone as core engines, with the Economic Development Zone, Feidong and Lujiang in support. The projects differ sharply in what they actually manufacture.

The Dingli Kesin joint venture deserves a separate note because it is the clearest local whole-body bet. Zhongding Shares, an Anhui auto-parts maker with four decades in rubber seals, signed into Baohe in February 2026; the JV combines Zhongding's parts business, LimX's humanoid control and Flexbot's electronic skin. Its first full-size humanoid came off the line in March 2026, about 23 days after signing according to local reports, with a planned annual capacity of 3,000 humanoids and roughly 200 units delivered by October 2026. The listed parent's broader Hefei robotics and parts center carries a reported total investment around RMB 1.2 billion. This is an auto-parts supplier turning itself into a robot-body maker — a pattern the Hefei supply chain is built to repeat.

What Hefei actually offers

Existing parts spillover. The city guides its six whole-vehicle makers and their appliance and panel supply chains to extend chip modules, sensors, motors, batteries and controllers into robotics. More than half of auto and robot components are described as shared; NIO, Volkswagen Anhui, BYD, BOE and their suppliers can pivot toward robot parts. The city has published a scenario-opportunity list of more than 100 items, matched to nearly 100 companies.

State capital and policy packages. The March 2025 "6128" program targets opening 60 OEM supply chains in a year, serving 100 companies on bottleneck problems, connecting the Huawei and iFlytek model brains, and eight scenario verticals. It sets up a RMB 10 billion future-industry fund, RMB 2 billion over three years for public service platforms, and RMB 100 million per year in special subsidies. Hefei municipal investment arms have backed Lingdong General, YouiBot and Zerith; the Xingtai holding brought in six embodied projects with RMB 2.17 billion in signed contracts. At the provincial level, Anhui's "ten-hundred-thousand" plan targets 10-plus national-leading companies, 100-plus key technologies and landmark products, and RMB 100 billion in full-chain revenue by 2027; a 2026 provincial opinion aims to put Hefei-Wuhu among the national front rank in industrial robots and the first tier in humanoids by 2030.

Platforms and a training ground. Hefei runs ten major R&D platforms, including the USTC Humanoid Robot Research Institute and the Jianghuai Frontier Technology Collaborative Innovation Center (Jianghuai Lab), whose Qijiang humanoid platforms have gone through several iterations. The Yaohai embodied-AI public training ground, invested by the city's investment arm at RMB 623 million, replicates 33 scenario types with 83 training robots across home, supermarket and industrial settings. Local parts makers sit alongside: Zhongke Lingxi, near Feixi, has shipped more than 1,000 dexterous hands across linkage, cable-driven and direct-drive routes; Flexbot supplies electronic skin; Sailisi makes C0-class miniature ball screws and planetary roller screws; Zhongding Ruisibo supplies harmonic reducers and six-axis force sensors with design nominations from humanoid customers.

Local natives and model layer. The homegrown roster includes Zerith Robotics, incubated with USTC and the Jianghuai Center, whose Zerith-H1 "Xiaomai" robot staffs small street shops; the company plans 500 robot shops in 2026 and 2,000 the following year, a company target rather than a delivered figure. Lingdong General, deeply incubated by iFlytek, took the LDB01 through what the High-tech Zone calls China's first industrial embodied-robot CR certification and later EU CE-MD. Jingsong Intelligent (688251.SH), a local listed warehouse-automation maker, grew its robot business revenue 71% in 2025; Heli and its Heli Yefeng unit build AGVs and wheeled-legged humanoid logistics machines with the Jianghuai Center; Juyi Technology works with the lab on auto-assembly robots. On the brain side, iFlytek's Spark X2 and HiDream.ai's world model feed local simulation and training work.

What has not been proven yet

The cluster's headline numbers should be read against where most companies actually sit. The city's own and media reviews describe most of the nearly 200 firms as still in fundraising, sample delivery or small-batch production; humanoid mass production, at a national level, has not been solved. Three distinctions matter for overseas readers.

First, project type. KUKA's Hefei center does flexible-system design, R&D, assembly and integration; its standard robot bodies are still built in Shunde. Yiyou Zhikong and Weiyi make joint modules and component arms, not whole machines. Some other entries are R&D, pilot or delivery centers rather than high-volume plants. A signed agreement or registered local subsidiary is not the same as a production line at scale.

Second, disclosed delivery. YouiBot's IPO route is not settled; EngineAI's Hefei PM01 delivery progress has no public update; several module suppliers quote capacity and planned output rather than shipped units. The cleanest evidence remains disclosed annual output, delivered-unit counts, named customers and certified overseas deployments.

Third, the bet itself is long-dated. Hefei's earlier wagers on NIO, BOE and ChangXin Memory returned over a decade. Whether the robot cluster grows a next-tier leader from this round cannot be answered from 2026 output alone; the local auto-parts conversion story and the warehouse-AMR base are further along than the humanoid whole-body story.

How Hefei fits against the other China robot clusters

For an investor, supplier or analyst mapping China's robotics base, Hefei slots in as the inland manufacturing-and-capital cluster rather than the model-design cluster. Shenzhen and Dongguan retain the tightest parts supply chain and the densest startup field; Hangzhou hosts Unitree and hardware-adjacent AI companies; Beijing concentrates model labs, institutes and policy seats; Shanghai draws embodied-AI research centers and foreign-backed integrators. Hefei's edge is lower-cost inland manufacturing, a matched auto/appliance parts base, open local scenarios, and municipal capital that writes early checks; its gap is model leadership, overseas service channels and the proven humanoid revenue that the whole national sector still lacks.

What to watch

  • Dingli Kesin delivery ramp. Whether the JV turns the planned 3,000-unit annual capacity into named customers and repeat orders, and whether Zhongding's listed filing discloses robotics-segment revenue separately.
  • YouiBot IPO. Which exchange and board the filing lands on, and whether disclosed semiconductor and energy customers convert into recurring mobile-manipulation revenue.
  • KUKA China center, 2027. Whether the Hefei innovation center reaches its year-one output target, and whether the Volkswagen Anhui relationship in the city produces integration orders for KUKA.
  • Humanoid yield across the cluster. The gap between announced capacity and delivered units at LimX, EngineAI, X Square and Leju; watch disclosures from district and municipal releases rather than launch-day claims.
  • Local parts scaling. Whether dexterous-hand, screw, e-skin and force-sensor makers in Feixi, Baohe and the High-tech Zone ship to multiple humanoid customers beyond a single local JV.

FAQ

Why are robot companies moving to Hefei?

Hefei combines an existing auto, appliance and panel supply chain whose motors, sensors, controllers and battery modules can be repurposed for robots; state investment arms and a RMB 10bn future-industry fund that co-invest early; and open local scenarios plus a RMB 623mn training ground. The city's earlier bets on NIO, BOE and ChangXin Memory established a track record of backing manufacturing clusters over a decade.

Is Hefei a humanoid mass-production hub yet?

Not yet. City figures put 2025 humanoid output above 400 units and industrial robots at 22,000 sets, but most of the nearly 200 companies are still fundraising, sampling or in small batches. Humanoid mass production has not been cracked anywhere in China, and several Hefei projects are module plants, integration centers or R&D offices rather than body factories.

Which listed robot companies have bases in Hefei?

Geek+ (2590.HK) runs its Lujiang manufacturing headquarters and sole global production base for heavy and high-speed picking robots. Jingsong Intelligent (688251.SH) is a local listed warehouse-automation maker. KUKA China, part of Midea, signed a smart-manufacturing innovation center in November 2026, but standard body capacity remains in Shunde. YouiBot is pursuing an IPO; the exchange and board are not settled.

What counts as a real Hefei robot factory?

Four project types appear in the announcements: finished-product plants (Geek+, the Dingli Kesin humanoid JV), component plants (Yiyou, Weiyi, Zhongke Lingxi), engineering-integration and assembly centers (KUKA China), and R&D/pilot/delivery centers. Disclosed capacity, annual output and delivered units are the evidence that separates a volume plant from a signed agreement.
