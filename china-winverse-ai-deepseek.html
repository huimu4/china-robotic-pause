Winverse AI: DeepSeek Leads Seed Round for Ex-Tencent Scientist Han Hu's Robot Brain Startup

DeepSeek has agreed to lead a tens-of-millions-dollar seed round in Winverse AI, a stealth-stage Chinese embodied-AI company founded in 2026 by former Tencent chief scientist Han Hu (胡瀚), Bloomberg and multiple Chinese financial outlets reported on October 12, 2026. Post-money valuation is approximately $400 million, with Meituan's Long-Z Investments also committing. The deal has not yet closed.

By China Robotics Insider · Oct 13, 2026 · Last updated: Oct 13, 2026 · 5 min read
Share on X LinkedIn
TL;DR
Winverse AI is the most high-profile Chinese embodied-AI funding story of October 2026. Founder Han Hu (Swin Transformer author, ex-Tencent chief scientist) raised a DeepSeek-led seed at a ~$400M valuation within months of founding. DeepSeek is not building its own humanoid — it is backing the model layer that will supply robot brains to Unitree, Fourier, UBTech and other Chinese hardware makers, mirroring OpenAI's bet on Figure AI.

The deal

According to Bloomberg, first reported on October 12, 2026 and confirmed by Caixin, Sina Finance, 36Kr, Pingwest and Yicai, DeepSeek has agreed to lead Winverse AI's seed round at a post-money valuation of roughly $400 million. The round size is described as "tens of millions of US dollars" — most likely in the $30-50M range. Meituan's venture arm Long-Z Investments (龙珠资本) has also committed capital. People familiar with the matter cautioned that the deal has not closed and valuation and investor list may still shift.

Who is Han Hu?

Han Hu is not a typical robot startup founder. He is the lead author of the Swin Transformer paper (2021), a landmark in computer vision whose hierarchical transformer architecture reshaped modern perception models. A Tsinghua PhD, he previously worked at Microsoft Research Asia and Baidu, and at Tencent he led the Hunyuan vision-language and embodied foundation model team as chief scientist. He left Tencent in 2026 to found Winverse AI.

What does Winverse AI actually build?

The company is still in stealth mode and has not publicly disclosed a product. The positioning, per Pingwest and 36Kr, is to build robot AI models — the software that lets a humanoid understand spoken instructions, reason about unseen environments, and execute complex multi-step tasks, as opposed to the low-level motion control that robot makers have historically handled in-house. In short, Winverse AI is aiming to be the "brain" layer, not the body.

One nuance from 36Kr: some investors view Winverse AI less as a full-stack embodied-AI company and more as a model efficiency and training acceleration play — which is exactly DeepSeek's home turf. The bet is that Han Hu, with his CV foundation-model background, can build VLA (vision-language-action) stacks that train and infer cheaply, the same way DeepSeek cut LLM inference costs.

The DeepSeek bet is a structural signal: rather than competing with OpenAI's Figure AI by building a humanoid itself, DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng has reportedly been meeting intensively with embodied-AI founders across China. The strategy is to supply the general-reasoning backbone (DeepSeek LLMs) and back the VLA application layer (Winverse AI) for the whole Chinese humanoid supply base — Unitree, Fourier, UBTech, etc.

What to watch

  • First product reveal. Winverse AI has not shown any robot demo. Expect a 2027 launch tied to a hardware partner — likely a Chinese humanoid maker rather than a DeepSeek-branded robot.
  • DeepSeek's embodied strategy. Liang Wenfeng's reported meetings with multiple embodied founders suggest more investments may follow.
  • Talent retention. Han Hu's team is drawn from Tencent Hunyuan, MSRA and Baidu; retention through the stealth phase is the near-term risk.
  • Closed round confirmation. The deal has not formally closed as of press time.

Its $400M seed valuation puts it immediately in the top tier of Chinese embodied-AI startups, on par with 1X and Figure AI's early rounds.
