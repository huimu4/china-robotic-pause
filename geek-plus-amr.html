Geek+ AMR: The Chinese Warehouse Robot Giant You've Never Heard Of

Geek+ (极智嘉) is the largest AMR (Autonomous Mobile Robot) maker in China — and one of the largest in the world — with 20,000+ robots deployed across customers including Decathlon, Walmart, DHL and Toyota Logistics.

Direct Answer: What Is Geek+?

Geek+ (Beijing Geekplus Technology Co., 极智嘉) is a Beijing-based AMR vendor founded in 2015 by Zheng Yong (郑勇), a former Amazon Kiva robotics engineer. It makes shelf-moving and tote-picking mobile robots for e-commerce, retail and 3PL warehouses. As of 2026, Geek+ has deployed over 20,000 robots across 30+ countries, with annual revenue in the hundreds of millions of dollars — making it the closest non-Chinese-rival to Amazon's Kiva fleet outside Amazon itself.

What Is an AMR? (Quick Refresher)

An AMR (Autonomous Mobile Robot) is a wheeled robot that navigates a warehouse floor using laser SLAM or visual markers. Unlike an AGV (which follows a fixed wire or magnetic tape), an AMR dynamically plans routes around people and obstacles. The most famous AMR deployment is Amazon's Kiva system, which moves entire shelves to human pickers.

Why Buyers Search "geek+ amr"

Outside China, Geek+ is still under-marketed — most warehouse-automation engineers know Locus Robotics and 6 River Systems but have not heard of Geek+, even though it deploys at a similar global scale. When a buyer types "geek+ amr" or "amr geek+" they are usually comparing Geek+ against Locus for a 3PL or retail deployment and want to know: (1) is it a real Chinese competitor to Kiva? (2) what are the specs? (3) can it ship to my region?

The short answer: yes, it is a real competitor. Geek+ has European and North American subsidiaries, speaks English, and has reference customers at Decathlon and Walmart — but most of its 20,000+ robot fleet is still inside China and East Asia.
