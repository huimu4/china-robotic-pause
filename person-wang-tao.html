Wang Tao

The reluctant genius whose crashed graduation project became the company that defined the global drone market.

汪滔

Founder, DJI

Founder of DJI · built from a 20 m² Shenzhen garage in 2006

DJIGlobal category kingConsumer dronesShenzhen

Who he is

Wang Tao is the reluctant genius of Chinese hardware. His university graduation project — a helicopter drone — crashed in a live demo, earning him a C grade and costing him his study-abroad plans. Professor Li Zexiang took him on as a master's student anyway.

In 2006, Wang founded DJI in a 20-square-metre Shenzhen garage. He was famously obsessive — sleeping in the office, refusing to ship anything that was not perfect. That stubbornness produced the Phantom, the drone that turned an aerial niche into a global consumer category.

Why he matters

Wang Tao did not just win a market — he created it. DJI's dominance is the single most important proof that China's robotics and hardware ecosystem can produce a global category king, not just a cheaper imitator. His path from crashed demo to world leader is the founding legend of Chinese consumer hardware.

Key facts

  • 2006: Founded DJI in a 20 m² Shenzhen garage.
  • Turnaround: Saved by professor Li Zexiang after a failed graduation demo.
  • Legacy: DJI built the global drone market, not just the Chinese one.
  • Proof point: A flagship of the XbotPark and Shenzhen hardware ecosystem.
