US-Listed China Robotics Stocks: XPEV, FFIE, HSAI and the 2026 Robot Stock Wave

US investors who want China robotics exposure have a surprisingly short list of direct tickers: XPeng (XPEV) with its IRON humanoid and a $900M robot funding round, Faraday Future (FFIE) pivoting into embodied AI with nine robots launching September 19, and Hesai (HSAI), the world's #1 robot LiDAR maker. Here is what each is actually doing — and the Unitree IPO cautionary tale that frames the whole sector.

By China Robotics Insider · Sep 14, 2026 · Last updated: Sep 14, 2026 · 8 min read

The answer · TL;DR
For direct US-exchange exposure to Chinese robotics in 2026, the list is short: XPeng (NYSE: XPEV) — its PX robot unit raised >$900M at a $6.3B post-money valuation (a China embodied-AI record) and the IRON humanoid reached automated production lines in September; Faraday Future (NASDAQ: FFIE) — pivoting hard into embodied AI, with nine robots launching and going on sale September 19 across humanoid, quadruped and wheeled-arm forms; and Hesai (NASDAQ: HSAI) — the global #1 robot LiDAR supplier (GGII Q1 2026). Baidu, Alibaba and Tencent are indirect plays via their robot investments, and UBTECH trades OTC (UBTRF) with a Hong Kong primary listing. The sector's cautionary tale: Unitree's A-share IPO (688836.SH) halved within 11 trading days. This is editorial research, not investment advice.

If an American investor wants to own the Chinese robot story, the menu is thinner than the headlines suggest. The buzziest Chinese robot companies — Unitree, AgiBot, UBTECH's main listing — are not on US exchanges at all. But three US-listed names genuinely carry robot stories, and two of them are moving fast enough that September 2026 is the month to track them. This is a field guide to those tickers, built only on verifiable public facts.

The field: who is actually tradeable

China's robot leaders are mostly private (AgiBot, Galbot, Fourier) or listed in Hong Kong and Shanghai (UBTECH, 9880.HK; Unitree, 688836.SH). On US exchanges, the direct and semi-direct options are these:

XPEV: the serious one

XPeng (NYSE: XPEV; HKEX: 9868) is the US-listed China name with the most substantial robot program. On August 24, 2026, its robotics unit (PX / Pengxing) closed a first funding round of more than $900 million at a post-money valuation above $6.3 billion — the largest single-round private financing ever recorded in China's embodied-AI industry. IDG Capital led, Gaorong Ventures participated, and Tencent and Alibaba each committed strategic investment, with He Xiaopeng's own affiliates subscribing $300 million. XPeng keeps control of the unit.

Two weeks later, on September 8, XPeng switched on what it calls the world's first automated production line for advanced general-purpose humanoids: an IRON unit completed automated final assembly and walked off the line under its own power, with more than 80% of core processes automated. Management targets mass production by end-2026, store and campus deployment in early 2027, and a global launch — including overseas sales — in 2027, at a monthly output of thousands of units. (See our full breakdown: XPENG's IRON walks off China's first automated humanoid line.)

FFIE: nine robots, one pivot

Faraday Future (NASDAQ: FFIE) is the volatile, high-risk version of the same story. Founder YT Jia, who built FF as an EV maker, has redirected the company into embodied AI (EAI) robotics. The product line already has a shape: at the NADA show in Las Vegas (May 2026) FF launched its first batch — the FF Futurist full-size professional humanoid, the FF Master athletic humanoid, and the FX Aegis quadruped — claiming to be the first North America-based company delivering both humanoid and quadruped robots. In late August it added the NextFuturist humanoid and Next Aegis quadruped.

On September 19 at 5:00 p.m. PT, FF holds its "Four-Core Full-Stack AI" launch: nine robot devices will be unveiled and put on sale, covering humanoid, quadruped and wheeled-arm forms across sizes, plus four industry productivity solutions (K-12 education, research, security, inspection) and its "one brain, many forms" EAI 2.0 ecosystem. The roadmap: a robot factory running this year, the first product off the line in February 2027, and a ten-year commitment of at least $5 billion across robotics, AI, advanced manufacturing and the industrial ecosystem. FF has also added a senior advisor focused on US government procurement. The caveats are as large as the ambition — FF has a history of dilution, delivery delays and regulatory risk, so treat this ticker as a speculative story, not a fundamental one.

HSAI: the quiet compounding machine

Hesai (NASDAQ: HSAI) is the least glamorous and most revenue-backed of the three. It is the global #1 in robot LiDAR (GGII, Q1 2026) and the #1 automotive LiDAR supplier in China (51% share as of February 2026, 13 straight months at the top). Its E1R solid-state LiDAR is designed for robots — lawn mowers, vacuums and humanoids — and it has locked in large-volume robot orders (a 1.2-million-unit agreement with KUMAR among them). For investors who want robot-sector exposure with an actual revenue line, HSAI is structurally different from XPEV and FFIE. See our full supplier analysis: China's LiDAR suppliers.

The cautionary tale: Unitree's IPO

No US-listed China robotics guide is complete without the sector's risk display of 2026. Unitree, the world's biggest humanoid shipper, listed on China's STAR Market (688836.SH, A-shares — not US) on August 19 at a 150.80 yuan IPO price. It opened at 1,100 yuan, up 629%, valuing the company at ¥444.9B — and then fell without pausing: below 850 yuan on day one, -16% on day two, below 550 yuan (a halving) by its 11th session, and below 500 yuan by September 14, roughly 55% off the peak. The lesson for anyone buying robot names anywhere: this sector prices in years of perfection, and valuations can halve in eleven sessions even for the strongest operator. US-listed China robot stocks carry the same exposure plus geopolitical and ADR-specific risk.

What to watch through Q4 2026

Three events now sit on the calendar: FF's September 19 launch (do the nine robots exist beyond renders, and does anything ship this year?); XPENG's end-2026 mass-production target (IRON volume versus Tesla Optimus's slip, per our production-line analysis); and Hesai's robot-LiDAR order book converting into quarterly revenue. Everything else is noise until those three resolve.

Frequently asked questions

Which Chinese robot companies are listed on US stock exchanges?

For direct exposure to China robotics on US exchanges, the practical list is short: XPeng (NYSE: XPEV) with its IRON humanoid and the PX robotics unit that raised over $900M; Faraday Future (NASDAQ: FFIE), which is pivoting into embodied-AI robots with nine products launching September 19, 2026; and Hesai (NASDAQ: HSAI), the world's #1 robot LiDAR maker. Chinese internet giants with robot exposure such as Baidu, Alibaba and Tencent trade on US exchanges as ADRs, and UBTECH is available on US OTC markets (UBTRF) though its primary listing is Hong Kong (9880.HK).

Is Unitree listed on US exchanges?

No. Unitree Robotics listed on China's STAR Market (A-shares) on August 19, 2026 under ticker 688836.SH. It opened at 1,100 yuan against a 150.80 yuan IPO price — up 629% — then lost half its value within 11 trading days and fell below 500 yuan by September 14, a drop of roughly 55% from the peak. US investors can currently access Unitree only indirectly through stakes held by listed vehicles.

What is Faraday Future's robotics business?

Faraday Future (FFIE) is pivoting to embodied AI (EAI) robotics. It launched its first robot line at the NADA show in Las Vegas in May 2026 (FF Futurist humanoid, FF Master athletic humanoid, FX Aegis quadruped) and added the NextFuturist and Next Aegis in August. On September 19, 2026 it plans to launch and sell nine robot devices covering humanoid, quadruped and wheeled-arm forms, plus four industry solutions (K-12 education, research, security, inspection). It targets a factory running this year and its first product off the line in February 2027, with a ten-year, $5B-plus commitment to robotics, AI and advanced manufacturing.

Did XPeng's robot business raise funding?

Yes. In August 2026, XPeng's robotics unit (PX / Pengxing) closed a first round of more than $900 million at a post-money valuation above $6.3 billion — the largest single private round ever recorded in China's embodied-AI industry. IDG Capital led, Gaorong Ventures participated, and Tencent and Alibaba each committed strategic investment, with He Xiaopeng's affiliates subscribing $300 million. XPeng retains control, and its IRON humanoid moved to automated production lines on September 8, 2026.

Are US-listed China robotics stocks a safe bet?

No equity is 'safe', and robot stocks are especially volatile. Unitree's A-share IPO halved in eleven trading days; FFIE has a history of dilution and regulatory risk; and robotics revenue is still tiny for every company above except Hesai.
