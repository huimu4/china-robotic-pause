China's Companion Robot Boom: Robot Pets, Hairy Companions and "Relationship" Androids

In 2026 the loneliness economy turned into a hardware business. China now fields the world's widest companion-robot product spectrum — from a ¥3,000-class robot pet to a 183 cm "companion" android with 88 degrees of freedom — backed by emotion-AI models, end-side privacy chips and the world's most complete robot supply chain.

TL;DR
Companion robots became China's most visible consumer-robotics bet in 2026. Global humanoid shipments hit ~18,000 units in 2025, up ~508% (IDC); China's emotion economy reached RMB 2.7T in 2025 (iiMedia); in April 2026 China logged 41 humanoid funding rounds, vs 16 a year earlier. The product wave spans five tiers: desktop robot pets (Loona/KEYi Tech), furry companions (Xinyan's Bubbo 1, ZTE-Nubia's iMoochi, Mind With Heart's An An), quadruped mascots (MagicAtom's MagicDog), full-size "relationship" androids (UBTECH UWorld U1, Eva.i, Moya, In2Mate's Jiaban), and child-growth companions (Chaoji Qiuqiu). The shared stack: VLA models, emotion models with long-term memory and personality states, motion+interaction+memory+touch pillars, data flywheels and end-side privacy (SoC+MCU+TEE). The open questions are commercialization ones: delivery and QC ("a moving doll"), 3C safety, privacy ethics, emotional dependency, and a missing regulatory framework — designers now preach "bounded companionship" that must not target minors or replace real social connection.

1. Why 2026 became the inflection year

Companion robots are being pulled by a demographic wave that is now officially a public-health issue. The OECD's 2025 Social Connection & Loneliness report found ~19% of people in 30 member countries lived alone in 2022 (up from 15% a decade earlier), with 31% of the over-65s living alone; people living alone were 3.9 times likelier than co-residents to report feeling lonely most or all of the time. The WHO has listed loneliness as a global public-health threat, comparing chronic loneliness to smoking 15 cigarettes a day.

The economics followed. In China, iiMedia's emotion-economy tracking puts the market at RMB 2.7 trillion in 2025, heading past RMB 4.5 trillion by 2029. Japan's one-person households exceed 38% (the "muen shakai" era), South Korea's 34%+, and the UK created a Minister for Loneliness in 2018. The pattern across markets: once single-person households pass ~30%, companionship products flip from novelty to durable goods.

On the supply side, 2025 is now treated as the year embodied AI left the lab, and 2026 as the small-batch delivery window. IDC put 2025 global humanoid-robot shipments at ~18,000 units, up ~508% year-on-year. Venture money is following: 2026 humanoid VC has already broken 2025's full-year record (Asia ~46% of the global total); China recorded 41 discrete humanoid funding rounds in April 2026 vs 16 a year earlier. Underneath, China's robot supply chain has pushed domestic reducer/joint-module localization to ~70% with ~40% cost reduction — the cost base that makes a "RMB 10,000-class" consumer robot conceivable.

2. The product spectrum: five tiers of innovation

2.1 Desktop robot pets — the affordable export tier

Loona Inc. (formerly KEYi Technology / 可以科技, founded 2014) is the consumer-robotics pioneer of the wave and its most successful exporter. Its Loona Petbot is a wheeled robot pet with a 2.4-inch LCD face that renders emotions, a 720p camera, a 3D time-of-flight sensor, a 4-microphone array and 1,000+ programmed movements — face recognition, voice commands and home navigation in a palm-friendly body. It lists around SGD 689 (Singapore store), and the line now includes a "Space Edition" and Loona DeskMate, an iPhone-powered desk robot. The company reframed itself in 2026 as "Loona Inc." — by your side, not at your command.

2.2 Furry and plush emotional companions

The fastest-growing niche is soft-bodied companions built around emotion, not motion.

  • Xinyan Group's Bubbo 1 (巴布) — a 65 cm plush robot that premiered at WRC 2026 in Beijing, marketed as "proactive-intelligence" family companionship with full-scene experience stations.
  • ZTE-Nubia's iMoochi — shown at MWC Barcelona (March 2026), an AI pet with circular OLED eyes that simulate emotion, a fur-like touch-sensitive body (sensors on head, back, nose and limbs) that reacts to stroking and cradling, an on-device AI chip for natural-language processing, voiceprint recognition to distinguish its owner, and five selectable personalities (Hopami, Mimiu, Cyn…). Chinese media frame it as a product for the lonely.
  • Mind With Heart Robotics (深圳无芯科技) — its emotion-AI panda An An (安安) won a CES 2026 award and joined the company's IFA 2026 Europe push (with Duncan 邓肯) targeting the European companionship market.

2.3 Quadruped mascots

MagicAtom's MagicDog (魔法原子, AWE 2026) is the consumer spin of the Spring Festival Gala's robot panda: claimed as the world's first "head-body linked" quadruped, blending audio, vision and touch interaction with head-and-tail movement for emotional expressiveness — the booth's "crowd favorite" at AWE 2026.

2.4 Full-size "relationship" androids — the highest-stakes tier

  • UBTECH's UWorld U1 series (launched June 30, 2026) — full-size hyper-bionic androids in male (183 cm / 42 kg) and female (168 cm / 35.2 kg) versions, both with 88 degrees of freedom, an "upbringing-style" emotion model, encrypted memory and customizable appearance. Media report a 10,000-unit order book. (See our U1 vs Optimus deep-dive.)
  • Yizhizai's Eva.i (苏妍) — Shenzhen startup targeting 35-55-year-old users with a "counselor-like, accomplished" persona researched from social-media engagement data; raised HK$1.26M from 129 backers on Kickstarter (1.6x a HK$780K goal) in 60 days, first overseas deliveries expected October 2026, ~400 seed users in private groups. Design details include cameras hidden in glasses/necklace accessories (not the eyes), and cheek skin that warms to 42°C to simulate shyness. Three further personas (CEO-type, idol-trainee, tan-skinned zoology student) are in development.
  • Zhuoyide's Moya (卓益得) — Shanghai, aimed at elderly-care companionship and premium reception; a softer, rounder face by design (deliberately avoiding "internet-celebrity aesthetics"), full-body tendon-driven actuation for slender limbs and smooth motion at only ~30 kg for safe close-range contact.
  • In2Mate's Jiaban (影子科技佳伴) — backed by HKUST professor Zhang Fumin; a full-size companion at a "RMB 30,000-class" price, with 16+ DOF micro-motor joints, on-device LLM voice (<800 ms response), cross-session long-term memory with a five-state emotion state machine, and self-developed mass-producible silicone skin holding ~36°C surface temperature.
  • Shouxing × "Against the Storm" (首形科技 × 逆水寒) — IP collaboration turning a game character into a full-size bionic robot for exhibitions, showing the IP-licensing route.

2.5 Child-growth and all-terrain companions

Chaoji Qiuqiu (超级球球), shown at CES Asia 2026, is an AI companion for children's emotional growth, character building and mental-health support, attracting distributors from Singapore, Korea, Japan, Germany, the UK and the Middle East. Separately, DOBOT LUMO (越疆鹿萌) launched August 5 as a claimed "first embodied all-terrain humanoid" — multi-modal emotion perception, 3D spatial understanding and proactive action with long-term self-evolution, aimed explicitly at the "RMB 100-billion-class companionship market." And OneRobotics (卧安机器人, Shenzhen) sells a platform play — a "one brain, many forms" OneModel across home service, health and emotional companionship — into 90+ countries with 500万+ households served.

3. The shared tech stack under the fur and skin

Strip away the aesthetics and 2026's companion products converge on the same pillars:

  • VLA end-to-end models — from Google RT-2 and Physical Intelligence π0 to Chinese flagship models, mapping vision, language and action into one latent space so robots act from instructions instead of canned motion libraries.
  • Emotion models — optimising emotional recognition, empathy, emotion state machines, long-term memory and personality consistency ("answering correctly" is the generalist benchmark; "feeling right" is the companion benchmark).
  • The four pillars of companionship hardware — motion, interaction, memory and touch, all four of which remain scarce in consumer products; In2Mate's Jiaban is a rare full-size integration.
  • Crossing the uncanny valley — via multi-modal coherence (vision+audio+touch+temperature at consistent quality) and "lifelikeness": Wulun Keji's bionic head robot An Ni cut lip-sync latency from 200-300 ms to under 50 ms with denser head actuators and Mandarin-specific mouth models.
  • Data flywheels and end-side privacy — companionship data is exclusive to devices that actually live in homes (uncrawlable, unbuyable, unlab-brewable), so winners defend it with on-device architecture: a "brain SoC + motion MCU + TEE secure zone" sandwich that writes privacy into the circuit board rather than the terms of service.

4. Business models: from selling hardware to operating companionship

The early money is a mix of crowdfunding (Eva.i's Kickstarter), pre-order batches (U1's reported 10,000 orders; In2Mate's limited pre-sale), exports (Loona, Mind With Heart's Europe push, OneRobotics' 90+ countries) and IP licensing (Shouxing × Against the Storm). The frontier model is the "hardware delivery + data operations" dual engine — a C-end home product validates emotion value while B-end institutions (elder-care homes, hotels, showrooms) provide cash flow and scale, and each delivery feeds the model that makes the next product more companionable. In2Mate structures this as four interlocking moats: engineering/supply chain → exclusive companionship data → companion model → end-side privacy architecture.

5. Risks and the open questions

For every product in the boom, there is a counterweight. China Newsweek's June 2026 investigation titled its cover story "Bionic companions, or expensive dolls?" — a fair summary of the gap between marketing and shipped reality:

  • Delivery and QC — supply-chain ramp, part yield and whole-unit consistency can produce stiff motion, rigid expressions and short battery life; delayed delivery invites refund disputes. "When the tech is immature, what you bought may be a doll that moves."
  • Physical safety — full-size bodies with many servo motors carry collision and pinch hazards; some prototypes lack complete 3C certification.
  • Privacy and likeness — camera placement, memory data handling (what is collected, what syncs to cloud, user visibility/deletion, breach accountability), and the risk of unauthorised facial replication as appearance customisation opens.
  • Emotional dependency — a permanently compliant partner can train users out of real relationships: "the lonelier, the more dependent on the machine." Designers now argue for bounded companionship: products should promote connection to the real world, and must not target minors for romantic-style attachment.
  • Missing regulation — no dedicated framework yet exists for embodied emotional robots: intimacy ethics, data supervision, vulnerable-user protection, modification/refit risk, market admission and testing standards are all unsettled.

6. Who will define the track

The consensus among Chinese analysts is that the companies most likely to define the next decade are Chinese — not because of the AI models but because companionship hardware is an engineering-and-supply-chain problem (silicone, joints, thermal management, skin materials, integrated at consumer cost), and China owns that stack. The realistic timeline is 3-5 years to maturity: small-batch validation in high-tolerance niches first (elder-care, premium reception), then diffusion into the mass consumer market. The bet, as Zhang Fumin put it, is modest and precise: technology cannot cure loneliness, but it can ease it.

Frequently asked questions

Which Chinese companies make companion robots?

The field spans four tiers: desktop robot pets (Loona/KEYi Tech), furry emotional companions (Xinyan's Bubbo 1, Mind With Heart's An An, ZTE-Nubia's iMoochi), quadruped mascots (MagicAtom's MagicDog) and full-size companion androids (UBTECH's UWorld U1, Yizhizai's Eva.i, Zhuoyide's Moya, In2Mate's Jiaban, Shouxing's IP robot).

How much do Chinese companion robots cost?

Loona's robot pet lists around SGD 689 (~US$520). In2Mate's full-size Jiaban is priced at a "RMB 30,000-class" level. High-end bionic androids (U1, Eva.i, Moya) sit far above that and are mostly pre-order/limited-delivery products.

Why is 2026 considered the inflection year?

Loneliness is now a recognised public-health issue (OECD single-person households ~19%; WHO compares chronic loneliness to smoking 15 cigarettes/day). China's emotion economy reached RMB 2.7T in 2025 (iiMedia), IDC put 2025 global humanoid shipments at ~18,000 units (+508% YoY), and April 2026 alone saw 41 Chinese humanoid funding rounds (vs 16 a year earlier).

What are the risks?

Delivery/QC ("a moving doll"), physical safety (3C certification), privacy (cameras, memory data, likeness rights), emotional dependency and social withdrawal, gender stereotyping, and a still-missing regulatory framework. Leading designers argue for "bounded companionship" — no minors, no replacement of real human connection.
