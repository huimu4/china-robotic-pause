DEEP Robotics' STAR Market Filing: The Robot Dog Company That Makes Money Everywhere Except Humanoids

Hangzhou's DEEP Robotics is the only Chinese quadruped maker whose audited numbers have been public twice — once for 2025, when it turned its first profit, and again for H1 2026, when it lost money while revenue doubled. Its prospectus is the clearest view yet of how the robot-dog business actually earns: an 85% grip on power-grid inspection, quadruped revenue shrinking as a share, wheeled-legs rising, and six humanoids sold in six months.

TL;DR
The filing: accepted by the Shanghai Stock Exchange on May 18, 2026, sponsor CITIC Construction Investment, seeking RMB 2.503B; at 18% new shares that implies a ~RMB 13.9B valuation. Status has been "under inquiry" since late May; the prospectus was updated on Sept 28, 2026 with H1 figures. Financials: 2024 revenue RMB 103M with a RMB 13.3M loss; 2025 revenue RMB 340M with a RMB 28.68M profit — its first; H1 2026 revenue RMB 259M (+117.67%) but a net loss of RMB 8.81M and a recurring loss of RMB 18.60M. Mix: quadrupeds 97.94% → 73.34% → 53.21% of revenue (2023 / 2025 / H1 2026); wheeled-leg Lynx M 22.11% → 34.89%; humanoids 6 units / RMB 2.79M / 1.08%. Moat: 300+ power-inspection units across 100+ State Grid and China Southern substations, an estimated >85% share of that niche and >90% in firefighting, with 79.3% of 2025 revenue from industrial applications. Use of proceeds: RMB 1.169B (46.7%) for embodied algorithms and models, 95%+ of that equipment budget for compute cards — more than 7x its entire three-year R&D spend of RMB 155M.

1. Who DEEP Robotics is

Founded in November 2017 in Hangzhou by Zhu Qiuguo, a Zhejiang University professor, DEEP Robotics built its name on the "Jueying" (绝影) quadruped line and became one of the "six little dragons of Hangzhou" alongside Unitree. Its product ladder now runs four steps: X-series industry quadrupeds (the X20/X30 class), Lite-series quadrupeds for research and commercial service, Lynx M (山猫) wheeled-legged robots, and the DR humanoid series — DR01 in August 2024, DR02 in October 2025.

Shareholders include JD.com and dozens of new investors who entered in the year before filing; Zhu holds 31.05% of voting rights. The products ship to 45 countries, with operations claimed across 50 countries and 1,200+ industry scenarios. Overseas revenue was roughly 20% in 2025.

2. The profit that appeared and then disappeared

The 2025 numbers were the sector's proof point: revenue RMB 340M, net profit RMB 28.68M, and an R&D ratio of 31.52% cumulated over three years. That made DEEP one of very few companies anywhere selling legged robots at a profit.

Six months later the profit was gone. H1 2026 revenue RMB 259M (+117.67%) — the fastest growth in its history — came with a net loss of RMB 8.81M and a recurring loss of RMB 18.60M, a wider gap than the headline figure because non-recurring items flattered the result. The mechanism is visible in the unit prices: the Lynx M's average selling price fell from RMB 197,600 to RMB 187,100, quadruped gross margin softened, and R&D kept climbing — 2025 R&D alone was RMB 84.3M, of which RMB 22.55M went to industry-grade humanoid development, the second-largest project that year.

This is the shape of the whole Chinese quadruped market in one income statement: volume up, price down, margin compressed, and the next product generation funded out of current gross profit.

3. What actually sells: the revenue mix shift

The most under-reported part of the filing is the mix. Quadrupeds are no longer the story — they are the base:

Two readings matter. First, the industry quadruped is the profit engine: 681 units at RMB 287,500 with a 54.35% gross margin is a genuinely good business, and it is the only line in the sector whose margin is disclosed. Second, the growth is coming from wheeled legs, not quadrupeds — 482 units in six months versus 377 in all of 2025, and a revenue share up almost 13 points. Wheeled-legged robots are cheaper to make, far more energy-efficient on flat industrial ground, and much easier to sell into factories and warehouses than stairs-climbing quadrupeds. That is why DEEP's product ladder is bending that way.

4. The moat: 85% of a niche nobody else wants to fight for

DEEP's defensibility is not brand or price — it is certification, integration and installed base in power inspection. The company has sold 300+ units for power inspection, deployed across 100+ substations operated by State Grid and China Southern Power Grid, including converter stations, wind farms, substations and water-hub tunnels, plus projects overseas. Third-party estimates put its share of that niche above 85% and its share in firefighting above 90%.

That concentration explains the revenue structure: 79.33% of 2025 revenue came from industrial applications (up from 69.29% in 2023 and 78.09% in 2024), with research/education and commercial service making up the rest. It also explains the customer list: State Grid, China Southern Power Grid, fire brigades — state and state-owned buyers, not developers.

The trade-off is real. Five largest customers were 43.19% of revenue in 2023, and heavy reliance on grid procurement means the business rises and falls with a single buyer's annual budget — State Grid's inspection-robot tenders are the single most important variable in DEEP's income statement. The company itself flags the need to reduce dependence on integrators and build direct end-customer sales and service capability.

The four projects are unambiguous about where the money goes:

The largest single line — RMB 1.169B for algorithms and models — is more than seven times DEEP's entire three-year R&D spend of RMB 155M, and 95%+ of its equipment budget is for compute cards. Read that as a statement of strategy: DEEP believes the next defensible layer is not the body but the VLA-style model trained on its own inspection data, and it intends to buy its way into that layer before rivals do. At 18% new shares, the raise implies a valuation near RMB 13.9B.

The valuation is the controversy. Chinese financial press calculated DEEP's issue P/E at roughly 3.2x Unitree's — defensible only if the profit is durable, which the H1 2026 loss puts in question.

6. The humanoid problem: six units

Here the filing is brutal about the narrative gap. DEEP sold one humanoid in all of 2025, then six in H1 2026 for RMB 2.79M (1.08% of revenue). Unitree shipped roughly 7,000 humanoids in the same half. DEEP's own risk section acknowledges the gap in shipment scale versus Unitree-class peers.

The strategy, though, is not to compete for the general-purpose humanoid market. DR02 was launched in October 2025 as the industry's first IP66-rated all-weather humanoid — dust-tight, resistant to heavy rain and high-pressure jets, operating from -20°C to 55°C, 175–180cm tall, ~78kg, 31 active degrees of freedom. The pitch is weather, not charm: a robot that can patrol a substation in a downpour, work a cold-storage room and then a hot workshop in the same shift. DR02 units ran live traffic-direction tests at Hangzhou's Xixi Wetland and downtown intersections during the National Day holiday, and a DR02-based robot traffic officer worked the 2026 Hangzhou Marathon.

That is a coherent "industry-grade humanoid" bet — and it is also why the units are expensive (~RMB 465,000 average, with configured units reported near RMB 1.09M to a university buyer) and rare. DEEP is not trying to win the humanoid volume race; it is trying to extend its inspection franchise into places wheels and quadrupeds cannot reach.

7. Risks worth watching

  • Price deflation across every line. X-series ASP was RMB 287,500 in 2025; the Lynx M fell below RMB 190,000; the Lite series sells near RMB 27,700. Volume growth is partly bought with price.
  • Customer concentration in state procurement. Grid and fire budgets are lumpy and cycled; a slow tender year shows up immediately.
  • The profit is one year old. 2025 was the first profitable year; H1 2026 already reversed it. A STAR Market listing on a 3.2x-higher P/E than Unitree needs a second profitable year to hold.
  • Review speed. Unitree cleared the STAR Market in 73 days; DEEP has been under inquiry since May 2026, with the exchange pressing on unit prices and humanoid volumes.
  • Compute spend risk. A RMB 1.17B algorithm program is a bet that inspection data plus VLA models produce a defensible software layer — if it does not, that capital becomes depreciation.

Frequently asked questions

How much revenue did DEEP Robotics make in 2025 and H1 2026?

RMB 340M in 2025 (from RMB 103M in 2024) with net profit of RMB 28.68M — its first profitable year. H1 2026 revenue was RMB 259M (+117.67%) with a net loss of RMB 8.81M and a recurring loss of RMB 18.60M.

Why profitable in 2025 but loss-making in H1 2026?

Revenue doubled while average selling prices fell across all lines (Lynx M from RMB 197,600 to RMB 187,100), gross margin softened and R&D stayed high — three-year R&D equals 31.52% of revenue, with RMB 22.55M of 2025's RMB 84.3M going to industry-grade humanoids.

What share of revenue comes from quadrupeds?

97.94% in 2023, 73.34% in 2025, 53.21% in H1 2026. Lynx M wheeled-legs rose from 22.11% to 34.89%; humanoids were RMB 2.79M (1.08%) on six units.

How strong is DEEP in power-grid inspection?
