China's Humanoid Actuator Suppliers: The Muscles of the Robot

Reducers make the joints; actuators make the motion. Here is who builds the "muscles" — Sanhua, Tuopu, Inovance, Kinco and more — behind Tesla Optimus and China's humanoid wave.

By China Robotics Insider · Sep 2, 2026 · 8 min read
Share on X LinkedIn

Searching for "China humanoid actuator suppliers" usually starts with a question about Tesla Optimus — and the answer is that China builds the muscles. Analysts estimate that without Chinese suppliers, the cost of Tesla's humanoid would be roughly six times higher. Actuators — the motors plus joint modules that make a robot move — are the single most valuable hardware category, and Chinese companies now lead the supply chain.

Three kinds of motors, one supply chain

A humanoid needs three actuator types, each with its own Chinese champions:

  • Frameless torque motors — for the big joints (hip, knee, shoulder, elbow). Kinco leads with ~50% domestic share; Wolong powers Unitree.
  • Coreless / voice-coil motors — for the dexterous hand. Moons' is the main Chinese name able to mass-produce high-performance units.
  • Servo / integrated joint modules — the "one-piece" actuator that bundles motor + reducer + encoder. This is where Sanhua and Tuopu win.

Sanhua (三花智控) — the muscle integrator

Sanhua started as the world's leader in refrigeration and EV thermal-management components — and turned that precision-manufacturing muscle into robot actuators. In October 2025, Musk placed a US$685 million order with Sanhua, making it one of the core suppliers of Optimus joint-module assemblies. It also supplies Xiaomi's CyberOne with precision reducers and joint parts, and CyberDog 2 with harmonic drives and micro liquid cooling — a rare supplier spanning both Tesla and Xiaomi.

Tuopu (拓普集团) — Tesla's hinge

Tuopu is deeply tied to Tesla — the EV maker accounts for roughly 35-40% of its revenue. Its factory in Mexico is now in production, with a capacity target of 300,000 sets by Q2 2026. Tuopu handles actuators and joint modules, effectively the "hinge" parts that let a humanoid bend its knees and shoulders at scale.

Inovance (汇川技术) and Kinco (步科股份)

Inovance, often called the "little Huawei of industrial controls," develops frameless torque motors and drive-control integration in-house and signed a supply agreement with Tesla reportedly worth over RMB 1 billion (~US$140M). Kinco is China's #1 in frameless torque motors (~50% domestic share), with 4th-gen torque density at 18.3 Nm/kg, and its collaborative-robot joint modules have entered global giants' supply chains — Q1 2026 sales hit 35,000 units (+246%).

Haozhi (昊志机电) and Moons' (鸣志电器)

Haozhi integrates harmonic reducers + frameless motors + force sensors into one-piece joints for quadruped and humanoid robots. Moons' is the main Chinese producer of high-performance coreless motors, the type used inside dexterous hands — a key supply chain if Optimus's hand scales.

From three parts to one module

The industry's biggest shift is the integrated joint actuator: instead of bolting motor + reducer + encoder together, suppliers now fuse them into a single module, with rotary joints pushing past 530 N·m and linear joints built around planetary roller screws. This favors exactly the companies that can control the whole stack — which is why Sanhua, Tuopu and Inovance are pulling ahead of pure component makers.

What to watch

  • Tesla order flow — the $685M Sanhua order and the $140M Inovance deal show who wins as Optimus scales toward 1 million units.
  • Mexico & localization — Tuopu's Mexico plant signals supply chains moving closer to Tesla's US assembly.
  • Hand actuators — coreless motors (Moons') and micro joint modules decide the dexterous-hand race.
