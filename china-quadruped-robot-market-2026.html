China's Quadruped Robot Boom: 90% of Global Shipments, Four Leaders and the Industrial Shift

Robot dogs are China's second robotics export after drones. In H1 2026 Chinese makers took roughly 90% of global quadruped shipments, and four companies — Unitree, AgiBot Kuat, Zhishen, DEEP Robotics — hold 76% of the market. But the leaderboard flips when you look at where the money is: industrial deployments have a different winner. Here is the 2026 quadruped map, with unit economics and the hidden project cost.

TL;DR
Market size: H1 2026 global quadruped shipments reached ~35,000 units (Counterpoint) to ~49,000 units, US$400M+ hardware revenue (IDC, +92.5% units / +95.4% revenue YoY). 2025 was 46,000 units; 2026 full-year is expected to exceed 90,000 units and ~US$1.5B, heading to US$10B by 2030 (60%+ CAGR). Leaderboard (all segments): Unitree 37%, AgiBot Kuat 16.3%, Zhishen 12.6%, DEEP Robotics 10.4% — 76% combined, China ~90%+ overall. Industrial-app leaderboard: AgiBot Kuat 27.8%, DEEP 26.0%, Zhishen 18.9%, Unitree 17.6%. Unit economics: consumer Go2 from ~RMB 9,000 (US$1,250), industrial units RMB 100k-400k, and a deployed industrial project typically costs 3-5x the robot's sticker price.

1. The numbers: two trackers, one direction

Counterpoint and IDC disagree on the level but not the trend. Counterpoint counts ~35,000 units for H1 2026, on track for more than 90,000 for the full year (vs 46,000 in 2025, ~US$700M revenue). IDC's count is higher — ~49,000 units and US$400M+ hardware revenue, +92.5% and +95.4% year on year — with professional applications at 19,000+ units (+260.5%) and US$270M+ revenue (+125.1%). Either way, H1 2026 alone nearly matched all of 2025.

The long-range curve is steep and consistent across analysts: 2024 ~RMB 1B revenue, 2025 ~RMB 2.3B, 2026 expected RMB 7.1B (~US$1B), 2030 projected RMB 53B / US$10B+ at a 60%+ CAGR, with annual shipments rising from ~60,000 units in 2025 to an estimated ~1.71 million in 2030, and average selling prices drifting down from RMB 38,000 to ~RMB 31,000 per unit.

2. Four leaders, two different games

Unitree (37%) — the scale leader. Its Go-series quadruped is the default research and entertainment platform worldwide, and its second-development ecosystem is the widest in the industry. Its quadruped business leans on consumer and education buyers — the segment that is roughly 60% of global unit volume but under a third of market revenue. The Go2 starts at roughly RMB 9,000, which is precisely why Unitree wins the unit count — and why it ranks only fourth in industrial deployments (17.6%).

AgiBot Kuat (智元酷拓, 16.3%) — the fastest riser. Spun out as AgiBot's own quadruped brand in August 2025, it went from zero to the global No.2 in twelve months, and it leads the industrial-application segment at 27.8%. Nearly 70% of its volume is B-end, with overseas markets at ~40% of shipments — a rare case of a Chinese robot brand selling industry first and entertainment second.

Zhishen (智身科技, 12.6%) — the quiet volume player, strong in inspection and patrol deployments; third in industrial applications (18.9%).

DEEP Robotics (云深处, 10.4%) — the industrial pioneer. Founded 2017 in Hangzhou, it is the only one of the four whose revenue is majority-industrial: 79% of its 2025 revenue of RMB 337M came from industrial applications, with 600+ enterprise clients in 50+ countries and autonomous substation inspection programs for State Grid. It filed for a STAR Market IPO in May 2026 and ranks second in industrial shipments (26.0%).

Two more names matter for different reasons: Micro Robotech (具微科技) ranks first globally in the manufacturing segment per IDC, built on extreme-environment verticals like electrolysis-aluminium plants where the robot must survive strong magnetic fields — and its units sell at ~RMB 300,000, roughly 30x a consumer dog. Weilan (蔚蓝科技) and Dobot's Rover X1 (RMB 7,499) represent the consumer end, where the robot dog is becoming a home companion and a jogging partner.

3. Shipments are not revenue: the unit-economics trap

Consumer and education models are about 60% of units but less than a third of revenue. An industrial unit priced at RMB 100,000-400,000 carries sensors, explosion-proofing, IP ratings and integration; and a fully deployed industrial project typically costs 3-5x the robot's sticker price once you add visible-light and thermal cameras, gas detectors, SCADA integration, on-site commissioning, training and a multi-year service contract. That is why DEEP and Micro Robotech, with far smaller unit volumes, can out-earn consumer-heavy rivals per unit — and why the industrial segment, at only ~40% of units, already represents about two-thirds of global hardware revenue.

4. Where the growth is: manufacturing and power

IDC's H1 2026 vertical split shows where industrial money actually lands:

The pattern: manufacturing is the fastest-growing vertical because the work is dangerous, repetitive and hard to staff; power is the largest installed base because State Grid standardized procurement early. Both are "inspection first" — the robot carries sensors, not tools. Tool-carrying (valve turning, sample collection) is the next capability frontier and is still early.

5. Why China owns 90%

The answer is boring and decisive: supply-chain depth. Motors, reducers, sensors and assembly are domestic; iteration cycles are short; and cost control lets one company sell at RMB 9,000 and another at RMB 300,000 from the same component base. The four leaders plus a long tail of 50+ Chinese quadruped companies cover every price band. Overseas, the reference players are Boston Dynamics and ANYbotics — technically strong, but commercialized largely through RaaS and far smaller volumes.

This mirrors the drone story: Chinese firms hold 70%+ of global consumer-drone shipments, and the quadruped market is following the same pattern of "born global" hardware — AgiBot Kuat already sells ~40% of its units overseas.

6. Can the share hold?

Three conditions determine whether China keeps 90%+, and all three are about moving from "cheap" to "trusted":

  • Core components and software — share bought with cost is held with performance and stability. The gap now is autonomy: most deployments are still teleoperated or rule-based patrol.
  • Repeatable vertical solutions — the shift from selling devices to selling capability; the winners will be those whose inspection programs can be copied from plant to plant.
  • Local service networks abroad — in overseas markets, service response and compliance often matter more than price.

The risk side is the same as the humanoid sector: consolidation. Counterpoint expects 2026 shipments to roughly double, but the unit economics of consumer quadrupeds are thin, and the industrial segment's integration burden favors a handful of players. A 50-company field selling into a market of 90,000 units will not all survive.

Frequently asked questions

How many quadruped robots shipped globally in H1 2026?

~35,000 (Counterpoint) to ~49,000 units with US$400M+ hardware revenue (IDC). Full-year 2026 is expected above 90,000 units and ~US$1.5B; 2025 was 46,000 units.

Which Chinese company leads the quadruped market?

Unitree at 37% of global H1 2026 shipments, driven by consumer/education (Go2 from ~RMB 9,000). AgiBot Kuat 16.3%, Zhishen 12.6%, DEEP Robotics 10.4% — 76% combined, China ~90%+.

Who leads industrial quadruped deployments?

AgiBot Kuat at 27.8%, then DEEP Robotics 26.0%, Zhishen 18.9%, Unitree 17.6%. Industrial projects cost 3-5x the robot's sticker price once payloads, integration and service are included.

How much does a Chinese robot dog cost?

Consumer/education from ~RMB 9,000 (Go2, ~US$1,250) or RMB 7,499 (Dobot Rover X1); industrial RMB 100,000-400,000+; specialized vertical units such as Micro Robotech's ~RMB 300,000.
