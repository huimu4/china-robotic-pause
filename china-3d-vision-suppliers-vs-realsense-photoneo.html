China's 3D Robot Vision Suppliers vs Intel RealSense & Photoneo: The Camera Inside Every Humanoid

While the actuator debate grabs headlines, a quieter land grab is settled: Chinese companies now make the eyes of most Chinese robots. Orbbec owns the service-robot and humanoid depth camera; Mech-Mind owns industrial eye-brain-hand cells. Here is how they stack up against Intel RealSense and Slovakia's Photoneo.

By China Robotics Insider · Sep 21, 2026 · Last updated: Sep 21, 2026 · 9 min read
Share on X LinkedIn
The answer · TL;DR
There are two distinct 3D-vision markets. In embedded RGB-D for mobile and humanoid robots, Orbbec (奥比中光) holds over 70% of China's service-robot 3D vision market and its Gemini cameras are a standard option across AgiBot, Unitree, UBTech and Leju — filling the vacuum Intel created by repeatedly scaling RealSense back. In industrial robot guidance, Mech-Mind (梅卡曼德), which listed on the HKEX in 2026 as the "first embodied eye-brain-hand stock," has deployed 29,000+ systems for 100+ Fortune 500 customers and competes directly with Slovakia's Photoneo, the structured-light precision leader. Chinese vendors win on price, software localization and supply; Western leaders still set the high-accuracy benchmark.

Two markets, two Chinese leaders

"3D vision for robots" covers two very different jobs. A wheeled or humanoid robot needs small, low-power RGB-D cameras for real-time navigation, obstacle avoidance and close manipulation — the role Intel RealSense popularized. A factory robot arm needs a high-accuracy, often fixed structured-light scanner plus grasp-planning software for bin picking, loading and inspection — the role Photoneo and Cognex occupy. China produced a category leader for each: Orbbec in embedded depth, Mech-Mind in industrial guidance.

Orbbec — the de facto Chinese RealSense

Orbbec (奥比中光, SSE STAR: 688322, pursuing a Hong Kong listing in 2026) is Asia's first mass producer of consumer-grade 3D sensor modules with its own depth-ASIP chips and close to 2,000 patents. The numbers explain its position: over 70% share of China's service-robot 3D vision market, more than 5,000 global customers, and its Gemini-series depth cameras are a standard perception option across the world's top-shipping humanoid makers — AgiBot, Unitree, UBTech and Leju. In Q1 2026 its domestic robot revenue tripled year-on-year and humanoid orders doubled sequentially, with lead times cut from ~40 to ~20 days.

The product line spans the Gemini 335/338/435 family (active structured light and active stereo, GigE/PoE industrial variants), a customizable 3D DLP camera reaching ~0.07 mm VDI/VDE accuracy and 0.05 mm Z-repeatability at 0.5 m, and robot-free data-collection hardware for physical-AI training. It is deeply integrated into NVIDIA Isaac/Jetson (the Gemini 435Le runs on Jetson AGX Orin), Windows, macOS, ROS and AMD ROCm — a deliberate RealSense-compatible ecosystem play, backed by dual manufacturing in China and Vietnam.

Mech-Mind — the industrial eye-brain-hand stack

Mech-Mind (梅卡曼德), founded in Beijing by Shao Tianlan and listed on the HKEX in September 2026 as the "first embodied-intelligence eye-brain-hand stock," sells the Mech-Eye structured-light 3D camera together with Mech-Vision, Mech-DLK and Mech-Viz software and complete robot cells for bin picking, machine tending, depalletizing and inspection. By its IPO filing it had deployed over 29,000 systems and served 100+ Fortune 500 companies — CATL, BYD, Foxconn, Midea, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, FAW and Tsingtao among them — across automotive, logistics, new energy, appliances and semiconductors. It raised over RMB 2B across 11 rounds (IDG, Sequoia, Meituan, Coatue, Intel Capital) before listing, and 78–86% of recent revenue came from existing customers — a sign of sticky, land-and-expand deployments.

Why the gap opened

  • The RealSense vacuum. Intel repeatedly reorganized and scaled back its RealSense group, leaving OEMs nervous about roadmap continuity — an opening Orbbec explicitly filled with pin-compatible, SDK-familiar hardware and aggressive platform integration.
  • Design-in density. When four of the world's highest-volume humanoid programs (AgiBot, Unitree, UBTech, Leju) all qualify a Chinese camera, the component becomes a de facto standard through volume and field data, not just spec sheets.
  • Software, not just sensors. Mech-Mind sells outcomes — a working bin-picking cell — which bundles the camera with vision software, deep-learning tools and robot motion planning. Photoneo sells best-in-class sensors and software too, but at European price points and with a narrower deployment base in Asia.
  • Cost and local service. As across the actuator stack, Chinese 3D vision typically lands at 30–60% below Western equivalents with local integration support — decisive for the thousands of Chinese factories automating on thin margins.

Where Western vendors still lead

Photoneo's parallel structured light remains the benchmark for high-quality 3D capture of moving scenes (where sequential structured light blurs), and its PhoXi line is rated for direct sunlight. Cognex and Keyence dominate the highest-end inspection and Western automotive accounts. The honest split: Chinese vendors lead on embedded depth, price, software breadth and deployment scale in Asia; European/American vendors still define the precision frontier and hold entrenched certification positions in Western automotive and aerospace.

What to watch

  • Orbbec's humanoid ramp — whether Gemini design-ins convert into multi-year per-robot content as AgiBot/Unitree scale to tens of thousands of units.
  • Mech-Mind post-IPO — whether the eye-brain-hand model expands from factories into logistics and humanoid manipulation data.
  • Vision-language-action convergence — depth cameras are becoming data-collection front ends for VLA model training (Orbbec's robot-free kits), tying perception vendors directly to the embodied-AI stack.

Frequently asked questions

Who makes the 3D cameras inside Chinese humanoid robots?

Orbbec is the dominant supplier: its Gemini-series depth cameras are a standard perception option across AgiBot, Unitree, UBTech and Leju, and it holds over 70% of China's service-robot 3D vision market.

Is Orbbec an alternative to Intel RealSense?

Yes. Orbbec's Gemini RGB-D and structured-light cameras directly target the RealSense form factor and SDK, with broad cross-platform support (NVIDIA Isaac/Jetson, Windows, macOS, ROS), while Intel has repeatedly scaled RealSense back.

How does Mech-Mind compare with Photoneo?

Both sell high-accuracy structured-light 3D cameras plus robot-guidance software. Photoneo leads on patented parallel structured light for dynamic scenes; Mech-Mind competes on a full eye-brain-hand stack, price and 29,000+ deployments including 100+ Fortune 500 customers.

What 3D vision technology do humanoid robots use?

Most combine active structured-light or active-stereo RGB-D cameras for close-range perception with optional LiDAR for mapping and an IMU; industrial arms use fixed or wrist-mounted high-precision structured-light scanners.
