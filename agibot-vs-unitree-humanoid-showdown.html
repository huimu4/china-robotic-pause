AgiBot vs Unitree: The Two Chinese Humanoid Giants, Compared

They are the two companies the rest of the world means when it says "Chinese humanoid." But they are not the same business. Unitree sells robot platforms — cheap, fast, and mostly to universities. AgiBot sells deployed work hours — to theme parks, retail chains and factories. In H1 2026 AgiBot out-shipped and out-earned Unitree for the first time; Unitree is the one with a public listing and the price war. Here is the head-to-head, with the three research houses' numbers reconciled.

TL;DR
Shipments (H1 2026): AgiBot ~9,700 units / 43% global share vs Unitree ~7,000 / 31% (Counterpoint); IDC ranks AgiBot No.1 above 8,600 units; Frost & Sullivan's unit list puts UBTECH (16,123) ahead of both, with AgiBot at 7,850 and Unitree at 4,900 — the three houses count different product categories and channels. Revenue (H1 2026): AgiBot close to RMB 2.0B vs Unitree RMB 1.152B and UBTECH RMB 1.269B. Unitree's 9M revenue was RMB 1.817B (+55.7%), Q3 RMB 665M (+69.9%), net profit down 17.4% on tripled R&D. Model: Unitree = hardware platform (73.6% of 2025 humanoid revenue from research/education; G1 from RMB 99k, R1 dual-arm RMB 26.9k); AgiBot = deployment fleet (300+ robots at Chimelong, 100 in Aishida stores, 17,625 QC tasks at 99.99% success). Capital: Unitree listed on STAR Market in Aug 2026 (~RMB 4.2B raised, ~RMB 61B valuation at issue, RMB 341.8B first-day cap); AgiBot is pursuing a Hong Kong listing.

1. Two companies, two theories of the humanoid

Unitree (Hangzhou, 2016, founder Wang Xingxing) began with quadrupeds and turned cost discipline into a global franchise. Its humanoid logic is a platform logic: make the body cheap and open enough that researchers, developers and integrators build on it. The G1 at RMB 99,000 in 2024 and the R1 dual-arm at RMB 26,900 in 2026 are the clearest expression — average humanoid selling price fell from RMB 593,400 in 2023 to RMB 166,400 in 2025, and then the R1 broke the floor again.

AgiBot (Shanghai, founded 2023 by Deng Taihua and Peng Zhihui) began from the other end: a full-stack AI company that treats the robot as a delivery vehicle for models. Its answer to "what is a humanoid for?" is not "a research platform" but a deployment state (部署态): a robot clocking paid hours in a real venue, measured by its own Worken metric (active robots × autonomous hours × output efficiency).

Notably, all five top slots on that table are Chinese — a fact that no amount of methodology difference changes.

3. Revenue and profit: the gap that matters

AgiBot: close to RMB 2 billion in H1 2026 — the highest of any general-purpose AI robot company and above Unitree's RMB 1.152B and UBTECH's RMB 1.269B. Its revenue path is steep: RMB 300,000 (2023) → RMB 60M (2024) → RMB 1.05B (2025) → ~RMB 4.5B expected for 2026. It had produced 20,000+ humanoids cumulatively by end-September 2026.

Unitree: 2025 revenue RMB 1.699B (+332.6%), split humanoid RMB 868M (51.8%), quadruped RMB 698M (41.6%), components RMB 104M. For 9M 2026: revenue RMB 1.817B (+55.7%), net profit attributable RMB 387M (+270.5%), but recurring (non-GAAP) profit RMB 349M (−18.8%) — the divergence is the story: R&D rose to RMB 263M (+191.5%, 14.47% of revenue) as the company pushed into world models and VLA. Q3 alone: revenue RMB 665M (+69.9%), net profit RMB 113M (−17.4%).

The difference in customer mix explains the margin gap. Unitree's 2025 1-9M humanoid revenue was 73.6% research/education, 17.4% commercial-consumer, 9.0% industry — labs pay for capability, not for uptime. AgiBot's revenue leans on deployment contracts where the customer buys a service level, which carries service cost but also repeat-order potential.

5. Capital: listed vs pre-listing

Unitree completed the sector's landmark IPO: STAR Market listing on Aug 19, 2026 at RMB 150.80/share, raising about RMB 4.2B on a ~RMB 61B valuation at issue. The first day closed at RMB 845, lifting the market cap to ~RMB 341.8B and topping out near RMB 444.9B intraday before settling around RMB 236.6B by late August — a valuation that prices 2027-2030 delivery, not 2026 earnings.

AgiBot has filed to list in Hong Kong. If it completes, the two will be directly comparable on public financials for the first time — the sector's real test. DeepSeek's RMB 140M strategic placement into Unitree (AI models, compute, general robots cooperation) adds a strategic layer to Unitree's "brain" ambitions that its financials do not yet show.

6. Risks: different, both real

Unitree's risk is structural: its revenue base is research buyers who are price-sensitive and not repeat buyers of the same unit. If the education/competition budget cycle slows, so does the platform story; and its listed valuation leaves no room for a miss. Price cuts (RMB 593k → 166k → 26.9k average/entry) compress gross margin exactly when R&D must rise.

AgiBot's risk is execution: deployment revenue is lumpy, service-heavy and hard to audit; the 99.99% success claim is vendor-reported on a single project; and theme-park and retail "deployments" must convert into repeatable industrial contracts to justify a Hong Kong listing. It also carries the sector-wide overhang: MIIT counted 140+ humanoid integrators in 2025, and at least 20 Chinese robot companies ran into distress in the past 19 months (Datar, Yinghe, Shenlan, Canshu and others).

The honest conclusion: they are not competing for the same customer. Unitree wins if the world buys robot platforms; AgiBot wins if the world buys robot hours. H1 2026 gave the first evidence that the second model can out-earn the first — and the next four quarters, with both companies' numbers public, will tell whether that holds.

Frequently asked questions

Which company shipped more humanoids in H1 2026?

AgiBot — Counterpoint: ~9,700 (43%) vs Unitree ~7,000 (31%); IDC also ranks AgiBot No.1 above 8,600 units. Frost & Sullivan's unit ranking puts UBTECH first (16,123), AgiBot 7,850, Unitree 4,900.

Which made more revenue in H1 2026?

AgiBot: ~RMB 2.0B vs Unitree RMB 1.152B and UBTECH RMB 1.269B. Unitree's 9M revenue was RMB 1.817B, Q3 RMB 665M (+69.9%).

How do their business models differ?

Unitree sells hardware platforms (73.6% research/education revenue; G1 RMB 99k, R1 RMB 26.9k). AgiBot sells deployment hours (300+ robots at Chimelong, 100 retail guides, G2 at 99.99% task success on one line).

Who is better positioned for 2027?

Depends on which model the market rewards: Unitree has public-market capital and the lowest prices but a lab-heavy customer base; AgiBot has deployment revenue and a pending HK listing but unproven margins. Both face a 140+ integrator field expected to consolidate.
