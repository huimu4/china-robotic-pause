KUKA's Heavy-Load Fortress: How Chinese Champions Are Attacking the Last Foreign Stronghold

China's industrial-robot market crossed a hinge in 2026: domestic brands passed 55% share (first time overtaking foreign), Estun took the overall No.1 with the first 10,000-unit quarter by a domestic brand, Fanuc slid to 9% and ABB agreed to sell out to SoftBank — while KUKA, Midea's fully-owned subsidiary, holds No.2 at 9.7% and more than half of the 300kg+ heavy-load segment. This is the story of that last fortress and the siege.

TL;DR
The 2026 battlefield: China's market sold 188,000+ industrial robots in H1 (+15%+); domestic brands hold 55%+ and own-brand exports passed imports for the first time. Rankings: Estun No.1 (first 10,000+/quarter domestic brand, six straight quarters No.1, eight years domestic No.1), KUKA No.2 at 9.7% (18,000+ units, +19%), Inovance tied ~10% with SCARA leadership; Fanuc fell from 20%+ peak to 9%; ABB sold robotics to SoftBank for US$5.375B. The dividing line is payload: below 20kg, domestic share is 58-85% by segment; at 110kg+ it is still only ~28% — and KUKA owns the top: >50% of China's 300kg+ segment (auto parts, energy-storage batteries, shipbuilding, aerospace), a China-designed 350-1,200kg heavy-load line with 100,000h MTBF, and Midea's component engine (reducer capacity +300k units/year). The siege is real (Estun's 1,200kg fully-autonomous heavy robot, Inovance's 37.2% servo share, prices at 60-70% of foreign), but heavy-load entry barriers — reducer fatigue life, long-run precision drift, plant-embedded software ecosystems — keep the fortress standing for now.

1. The battlefield: a 188,000-unit half-year

MIR Ruigong counts 188,000+ industrial robots sold in China in H1 2026, up over 15% year-on-year, with full-year growth still expected in double digits; GGII projects 380,000+ units for 2026 (+11%) and 620,000+ by 2030. Growth is broad: every downstream except solar grew, led by electronics-semiconductors, lithium batteries, automotive-electronics and auto parts; SCARA shipments rose 17%+ on smartphone/AI-server capacity; 6-axis below 20kg grew 14%+ on cleanroom models; and — the number that matters for this story — 6-axis above 20kg grew 7%, while the 300kg+ heavy-load tier grew 12%+, the fastest-growing premium pocket in the market.

The structure flipped in 2026: domestic brands passed 55% share in Q1 (first time above foreign brands), and China's own-brand robot exports exceeded imports for the first time, reaching 156 countries. Five years ago the "big four" (Fanuc, ABB, Yaskawa, KUKA) held over 60% of China; they now hold ~40%.

2. The ranking war: Estun first, KUKA second — and everyone else

  • Estun (埃斯顿): the new No.1. H1 2026 shipments ranked first across all brands for the sixth consecutive quarter, with the first-ever 10,000+ unit quarter by a domestic brand (Q2) and eight straight years as China's domestic leader. Revenue RMB 2.578B (+1.1%) but profit inflected hard: net profit +2,314% to RMB 161M, gross margin 31.81% (+4.17pct). Overseas revenue RMB 936M (+25%, 36.3% of total) via 75 service points; it became China's first A+H-listed robot maker (HKEX, March 2026).
  • KUKA: the No.2 at 9.7%. H1 2026: 18,000+ units (+19%), 9.7% share (MIR), up to second place — the highest-ranked foreign brand in China. Its group revenue grew 10%+; China contributed over 30% of KUKA revenue. KUKA shipped 30,000+ units in full-year 2025 alongside Estun and Fanuc.
  • Inovance (汇川): the silent No.1-tier contender. ~10% overall share in Q1 2026 (tied first tier), No.1 in SCARA at 27%, and a machine-tool engine that no robot maker matches: servo share 37.2%, inverter 21.4%, small-PLC 14.8% (all records) on H1 revenue of RMB 24.675B (+20.3%). 2025 full-year shipments passed 20,000 units.
  • Fanuc: from 20%+ to 9%. Still China's most profitable foreign robot house (FY2025 group revenue ¥857.8B/+7.6%, 21.4% operating margin) and growing again in China (+21% revenue, +55% orders in Q1), but relative share keeps sliding.
  • ABB: the exit. In October 2025 ABB agreed to sell its robotics business to SoftBank at US$5.375B enterprise value (robotics was 7% of group revenue with a 12.1% EBITA margin), closing expected mid-late 2026 — the single most symbolic retreat of the old guard.

3. KUKA's fortress: the heavy-load moat under Midea

KUKA's defense-in-depth has four layers, and all of them sit in the 300kg+ segment where it holds over 50% of China's market (MIR, H1 2026), across auto parts, energy-storage batteries, shipbuilding and aerospace:

  • China-designed heavy-load line. The "350-1,200kg high-performance heavy-load robot forward-design" program (Midea's Lancer Lab national key lab + KUKA Guangdong + Guangdong Jiya Precision) passed CAS-level appraisal at "internationally advanced" level: 100,000-hour MTBF reliability, four new models (350E, 550E…) already in volume use in new-energy and aviation manufacturing.
  • Global flagship for the top end. KR TITAN ultra Gen-2 (1,500kg long-reach heavy handling) plus KR CYBERTECH 25/35kg additions and the KMP 1000i-PT omniMove narrow-aisle forklift — a full product matrix across 20+ industries from heavy to light.
  • Midea's component engine. H1 2026: 26,000 precision reducers and 58,000 heavy-load servo motors shipped by Midea Industrial Technology; a new Shunde base will add 300,000 units/year of reducer capacity — self-supply that directly attacks the cost base of domestic challengers' imported components.
  • Brand + installed base. Shunde local manufacturing gives "local brand" pricing with German engineering credibility; KUKA's China stage presence (CCTV Spring Festival Gala robotics routines) and Midea's 750 robots per 10,000 employees showcase factory keep the brand inside China's manufacturing story.

4. The siege: how Chinese champions attack

  • Payload escalation. Estun's 1,200kg super-heavy robot achieves 100% self-developed core components (dual-motor synchronous control, dual-reducer hard-sync) — the same attack line as its iER130-3200 (130kg/3.2m) six-axis. Inovance converts servo dominance into robot cost advantage. Domestic six-axis share already reaches 73.6% below 20kg.
  • Price. GGII data: SCARA fell to ~RMB 10,000-class for automotive/3C accounts (-50% in a year), 6kg six-axis to ~RMB 20,000+, some cobots below RMB 20,000. Equivalent domestic bodies price at 60-70% of foreign; maintenance runs ~half; payback windows compressed from 3-5 years to <18 months in standardized lines.
  • AI + full-stack. Estun's iER.OS / RoboBase / Juliet robot language, the Kuzhuo acquisition (cobots + embodied), and industrial AI welding — shifting competition from hardware specs to data-and-workflow ecosystems, where incumbents' software moats are the next target.
  • Rationalization. Estun says the market is moving from "irrational price war" toward rational high-end competition — precisely the segment (high-value, quality-sensitive) where foreign brands still lead, and where Estun now aims its "high-end localization" push.

5. Why the fortress still holds

The heavy-load gate is defended by physics and installed systems, not marketing. Third-party estimates put domestic share at only ~28% for 110kg+ robots: reducer fatigue life still trails (international ~10,000h vs domestic-head ~7,200h measured), long-run repeat-precision drift widens after thousands of hours, and — decisively for auto plants — the software/process stack (KUKA.Sim, Fanuc's ROBOGUIDE, ABB's RobotStudio) carries decades of process libraries with firmware-identical virtual controllers, making brand switching on existing lines a full-ecosystem migration. New-plant projects switch far more easily, which is why the 300kg+ growth (+12%) is where the battle will be decided.

The old guard is also not standing still on AI: rather than build humanoids, they are bolting AI onto their arms — ABB fused RobotStudio with Omniverse, Fanuc linked ROBOGUIDE with NVIDIA IsaacSim (joining the Cosmos physical-AI alliance with Yaskawa), and KUKA launched its own AMP platform for AI integration. The thesis: industrial customers pay for ROI and uptime, not form factors.

6. The verdict: a two-front war

For KUKA, the scoreboard is healthy but the trajectory is the problem: No.2 share (9.7%) is up, but Midea's Robotics & Automation segment (RMB 16.621B, +10.3%) runs at a 20.9% gross margin that fell 1.86pct year-on-year — revenue grows, profit lags, as price pressure seeps upward from below. For Estun and Inovance, volume is won; the prizes left are heavy-load margin and the energy-storage/shipbuilding/aerospace accounts now being fought over. The realistic forecast: 3-5 more years before the 300kg+ tier sees the same 55% domestic crossing that the overall market just recorded — unless Midea's component scale-up (300k reducers/year) and KUKA's China-designed heavy line concede cost on the top tier first.

Frequently asked questions

Who is No.1 in China's industrial-robot market in 2026?

Estun — first across all brands in H1 2026 (six straight quarters), with the first 10,000+ unit quarter by a domestic brand and eight years as China's domestic No.1. KUKA is No.2 at 9.7%.

How is KUKA defending against Chinese rivals?

It holds >50% of China's 300kg+ segment (auto parts, energy storage, shipbuilding, aerospace); localizes under Midea — Shunde manufacturing, a China-designed 350-1,200kg line at 100,000h MTBF, and Midea's component engine adding 300k reducers/year of capacity.

What is the domestic vs foreign share now?

Domestic brands passed 55% in early 2026 (first time above foreign); own-brand exports exceeded imports. The big four fell from >60% five years ago to ~40%; ABB agreed to sell robotics to SoftBank for US$5.375B.

Why is heavy-load hard for Chinese brands?

Domestic share at 110kg+ is only ~28% (2025, third-party estimate): reducer fatigue life (~7,200h vs 10,000h), long-run precision drift, and plant-embedded software ecosystems (KUKA.Sim/ROBOGUIDE/RobotStudio) that make brand switching on existing lines expensive.
