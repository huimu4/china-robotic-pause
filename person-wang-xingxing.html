Wang Xingxing

The post-90s founder who turned cost-engineered quadrupeds into the world's best-selling humanoid robots.

Who he is

Wang Xingxing is the post-90s founder who turned cost-engineered quadrupeds into the world's best-selling humanoids. His company, Unitree, started with robot dogs priced far below competitors, then used that cost discipline to dominate humanoids.

The results speak in numbers: Unitree shipped more than 5,500 humanoid robots in 2025 — the global leader. Its G1 became the first mass-produced humanoid to cross 10,000 units. In 2026 the H1 was clocked sprinting at 10 m/s, near human record pace. The company filed for a Star Market IPO that cleared in a record 73 days.

Why he matters

Wang's bet is vertical integration: build your own motors, actuators and control software, and you can price humanoids where rivals cannot follow. He represents a distinctly Chinese playbook — brutal cost engineering applied to the most ambitious machine category on earth — and he is currently winning it.

Key facts

  • 2025: 5,500+ humanoid robots shipped — global leader.
  • G1: First mass-produced biped humanoid to cross 10,000 units.
  • 2026: H1 sprinted at 10 m/s; target of 10,000–20,000 humanoid shipments.
  • IPO: Star Market filing cleared in a record 73 days.
