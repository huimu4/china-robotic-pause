China's AgiBot Built 20,000 Humanoid Robots — More Than the Whole World Sold in 2025

On September 24, 2026, Shanghai-based AgiBot (智元机器人) handed over its 20,000th humanoid robot to Chimelong Group — and with it, 300 units that now staff the world's first embodied-AI theme park on Hengqin Island. For context: the International Federation of Robotics recorded roughly 7,000 humanoid robots sold across the entire planet in 2025. One Chinese company has now produced nearly three times that number in nine months.

The answer · TL;DR
On September 24, 2026, AgiBot (智元机器人, Shanghai) delivered its 20,000th humanoid robot to Chimelong Group. Simultaneously, 300+ AgiBot robots began regular operations at Chimelong Spaceship theme park on Hengqin Island, Zhuhai — the world's first large-scale embodied-AI theme-park deployment. The IFR recorded only ~7,000 humanoid robot sales globally in all of 2025. AgiBot went from 10,000 units produced in March 2026 to 20,000 in September — doubling output in six months. The gap between Chinese production volume and Western pilot-scale deployments (Tesla Optimus, Figure, Boston Dynamics) is now the defining fact of the humanoid race.

The number that matters is not 20,000. It is the denominator. The International Federation of Robotics' 2026 report records roughly 7,000 humanoid robots sold worldwide in 2025 — a year when Tesla, Boston Dynamics, Figure, Agility and every other Western humanoid program combined could not push past four figures. By September 2026, a single Shanghai startup has manufactured nearly three times that number. This is not a press-release milestone; it is a production-line fact, and it changes the frame of the entire humanoid race.

What actually happened on September 24

AgiBot and Chimelong Group launched the first phase of a large-scale embodied-AI deployment at Chimelong Spaceship (长隆宇宙飞船) on Hengqin Island, Zhuhai. More than 300 AgiBot robots are integrated into the park's daily operations — not as exhibits behind glass, but working alongside human staff in entertainment, science education, guided tours and guest services. The 20,000th unit rolled off AgiBot's production line specifically for this handover, linking a manufacturing milestone with one of the company's largest real-world deployments.

The two companies also unveiled a joint Embodied AI Cultural Tourism Research Institute at the park — a signal that the deployment is not a marketing stunt but the first of a planned pipeline of humanoid applications in tourism, hospitality and public spaces.

China vs. the West: the volume gap is now the story

The Western humanoid programs — Tesla Optimus, Figure 02/03, Boston Dynamics Atlas, Agility Digit — are betting on capability: better on-board AI, better manipulation, better autonomous task planning. They have deliberately kept volumes low while the software matures. Tesla has said it expects to begin Optimus production at Fremont in early 2026; Figure has run pilot deployments with BMW and other customers but has not disclosed a five-figure production run.

China is running the opposite playbook. Unitree, Ubtech, Fourier, AgiBot and a dozen others are treating the humanoid as a hardware problem first: build it at scale, put it in front of real customers, iterate on the software from field data. The Chimelong deployment is the logical endpoint of that strategy — 300 robots collecting operational data in a real public space every day, which no Silicon Valley pilot can match.

Why a theme park matters

The choice of Chimelong — rather than a factory — is deliberate. A theme park is the ideal humanoid proving ground: high foot traffic, unscripted human interaction, repetitive service tasks (greeting, directing, photo ops), and a customer base that expects novelty rather than perfection. It is also a venue where a robot that fails gracefully is more acceptable than one on a factory floor where a dropped part costs real money.

If AgiBot can run 300 robots reliably in a theme park for 12 months, it has a reference case for every mall, airport, hospital and hotel in China — and increasingly, in Southeast Asia and the Middle East, where Chinese humanoids are already being trialed. The tourism use case is the wedge; warehouses and factories are the follow-on.

What to watch next

  • AgiBot's 30,000-unit target — the company has signaled it aims to cross 30,000 units by early 2027. If it holds the current curve, that target is credible.
  • Tesla Optimus Fremont production — Elon Musk has said Optimus production starts in early 2026. Whether Tesla hits four figures or stalls at pilot volumes will tell us if the West can close the volume gap.
  • Unitree's H2 pricing — at ~$16,000, Unitree is already the price leader; AgiBot's next-generation model is expected to compete in the same band.
  • Chimelong operational data — 300 robots in a real public space for 12 months will produce the largest humanoid field-dataset in the world. Whoever owns that data owns the next generation of humanoid AI.

Frequently asked questions

How many humanoid robots has AgiBot built?

AgiBot (智元机器人) delivered its 20,000th humanoid robot on September 24, 2026, to Chimelong Group for its theme park in Zhuhai. The company had produced 10,000 units as of March 2026 — meaning it doubled output in six months.

How many humanoid robots were sold globally in 2025?

The International Federation of Robotics (IFR) reported that only about 7,000 humanoid robots were sold worldwide in all of 2025. AgiBot's 20,000 units produced by September 2026 is therefore nearly three times the entire global humanoid sales volume of 2025.

What is the AgiBot Chimelong theme park deployment?

On September 24, 2026, AgiBot and Chimelong Group launched the world's first large-scale embodied-AI theme park at Chimelong Spaceship on Hengqin Island, Zhuhai. More than 300 AgiBot robots work alongside human staff in entertainment, science education, guided tours and guest services. The two companies also established a joint Embodied AI Cultural Tourism Research Institute.

Who founded AgiBot (智元机器人)?

AgiBot was founded in 2023 by Peng Zhihui (彭志辉), known online as Zhihuijun (稚晖君), a former Huawei algorithm engineer and Bilibili science influencer. The company is based in Shanghai and has become one of China's highest-valued humanoid robot startups.

How does AgiBot compare to Tesla Optimus and Figure?

Tesla expects to begin Optimus mass production at Fremont in early 2026; Figure has deployed limited pilot units with BMW and other customers. AgiBot's 20,000 units — produced and delivered, not just announced — means China currently leads the world in humanoid robot production volume by a wide margin, though Western rivals argue their units have higher on-board AI capability.
