People · The Pioneers

Jiang Xinsong

The founding figure of the entire industry — the man who proposed building robots in China in 1972, when almost no one took the idea seriously.

蒋新松

The Father of Chinese Robotics · 1931–1997

Chief scientist of China's 863 Program automation domain · namesake of SIASUN (新松)

1931–1997Founding figureShenyang Institute of Automation

Who he was

In 1972, at a time when almost no one in China took robotics seriously, Jiang Xinsong proposed building robots. It was a bold, almost lonely idea — but he spent the next two decades proving it. His team completed China's first industrial robot in 1980, then built China's first 6,000-metre untethered underwater vehicle, pushing the country's robotics capability from zero to something real.

Jiang served as chief scientist of the automation domain of the national 863 Program — the state science initiative that would fund and coordinate much of China's early high-tech research. From that position he nurtured a generation of researchers, including the students who would later carry robotics from the lab into the market.

Why he matters

Jiang Xinsong is the root of China's robotics family tree. Nearly every early institution and founder in this industry traces back to his lab and his students. He died of overwork in 1997 — three years before the company that would carry his legacy, SIASUN (新松), was founded in his name.

Key facts

  • 1980: His team completed China's first industrial robot.
  • Chief scientist: Automation domain of the national 863 Program.
  • Legacy: SIASUN, China's first robotics company, carries his name (新松).
  • 1931–1997: He died three years before SIASUN was founded.
